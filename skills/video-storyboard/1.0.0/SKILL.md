# Video Storyboard
## When to use
Use before video generation or production to define a finite visual sequence and continuity rules.
## Required inputs
Creative direction, narrative goal, source assets, duration, aspect ratio, and required messages.
## Step sequence
1. Define beginning, development, and payoff. 2. Break into timed shots. 3. Specify subject/camera/action per shot. 4. Add transition and continuity rules. 5. Validate total duration.
## Design heuristics
Each shot should advance information or emotion; use motion and cuts deliberately rather than continuously.
## Constraints
Keep shot count and total duration bounded and preserve protected product/brand content.
## Verification checklist
Check timing sum, continuity, narrative clarity, asset feasibility, and final message visibility.
## Failure modes
Unbounded shot lists, continuity errors, impossible camera movement, and style changes between shots.
## Examples
Synthetic example: a 10-second fictional beverage film uses four shots: macro condensation, hand pickup, pour, and clean end frame.
## What not to do
Do not use a storyboard as permission to invent unsupported claims or brand elements.
