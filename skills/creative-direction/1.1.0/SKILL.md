# Creative Direction
## When to use
Use after strategy to lock a traceable visual thesis before downstream generation or layout work.
## Required inputs
Brand strategy, available assets, source-grounded references, and non-negotiable constraints.
## Step sequence
1. Define one visual thesis. 2. Map it to composition, typography, imagery, color, and motion. 3. Mark reference evidence versus invention. 4. Define acceptance and rejection criteria. 5. Record intentional degrees of freedom.
## Design heuristics
Favor a small number of strong, testable principles with visible hierarchy and deliberate contrast.
## Constraints
Preserve asset rights, avoid reference cloning, and keep uncertain assumptions explicit.
## Verification checklist
Trace each principle to strategy, verify references are evidence rather than templates, and confirm downstream teams can make consistent choices.
## Failure modes
Style soup, reference imitation, untestable adjectives, and directions that conflict with fixed brand constraints.
## Examples
Synthetic example: a fictional mobility brand uses “quiet velocity”: long horizontal rhythm, restrained type, sparse luminous accents, and documentary-scale imagery.
## What not to do
Do not treat popular visual trends as a substitute for strategic rationale.
